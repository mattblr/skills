# Signature motion ideas

Choose a gesture that makes sense for the mark's construction. These are starting points, not a required set of states. Timing ranges below are suggestions; test them at the intended size.

| Gesture | Works well with | Movement and trigger |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shoulder peek | Overlapping shapes or a repeated motif | A concealed part leans out after a short pause, holds for recognition, then tucks away. Use a 600 to 900 ms reveal and a longer rest. Keep the front silhouette calm. |
| Paper fold | Angular marks, ribbons, folded corners | One corner opens as the character becomes curious, then folds back. Preserve the crease and drawing order so the geometry feels attached. A hover or focus reaction can take about 400 ms. |
| Soft landing | Rounded or cushion-like forms | A small upward hop ends in a brief squash and recovery. Trigger once on success, around 600 to 800 ms total. Anchor the bottom edge during compression. |
| Passing pulse | Segmented marks, linked nodes, repeated bars | A small expansion travels from one segment to the next during a wait. Use a low amplitude and a clear pause between passes. Stop on the resting pose when work ends. |
| Periscope | A stem, notch, neck, or tall counter | A feature extends slightly, looks toward an event, and retracts. Use for discovery or a greeting. Keep it connected to the body throughout. |

Use pauses to give a gesture shape. A character that moves all the time is hard to place beside a conversation or a table. Keep an entrance or celebration finite; show a persistent state through posture or expression.

For reduced motion, choose a still pose that carries the same meaning. A folded corner may stay open for curiosity; a waiting character can remain attentive while a nearby text label explains the state.
